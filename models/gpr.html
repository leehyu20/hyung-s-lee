About the Virtual Laboratory

The laboratory uses two-dimensional (2D) Finite-Difference Time-Domain (FDTD) electromagnetic simulations to generate synthetic GPR A-scans and B-scans under controlled pavement conditions. The simulations use a \(TM_z\) formulation and Convolutional Perfectly Matched Layers (CPML) at the computational boundaries. Experiments to date focus on ground-coupled GPR with a nominal 1.5-GHz antenna configuration.

What Can the Laboratory Explore?

  • Layered pavement systems: Interfaces, thickness changes, irregular boundaries, and thin layers.
  • Cracks and water intrusion: Dry-crack progression and changes in response as water enters a crack.
  • Heterogeneous materials: Explicit larger aggregate particles, effective fines–pore matrices, and air voids.
  • Asphalt compaction: Evolving aggregate arrangements, decreasing air voids and AC thickness, and changes in surface-reflection amplitude.
  • Moisture and groundwater: Spatially varying initial moisture and rising groundwater with a simplified capillary fringe.
  • And more to come!

Scope and Future Directions

This is a research simulation framework, not a publicly released, general-purpose GPR software package. These are two-dimensional numerical simulations designed for controlled investigations, rather than field-validated GPR measurements.

Future investigations may include different antenna configurations—especially air-launched GPR—along with antenna frequency, material variability, and signal-processing approaches.
